# Model selection

This policy owns task organization, model and execution-route selection, chats, workspaces, Goals, collaboration, and continuity through acceptance, succession, and task closure. Project adoption follows the [rule ownership requirements](../AGENTS.md#code-preferences).

## Stable principles

- Select a model by the business judgment, responsibility boundaries, impact, and uncertainty the work involves. File counts and lines of code do not measure that burden.
- For complex work, prefer one task or domain lead continuously responsible for understanding the business, designing responsibilities, settling key judgments, and accepting the result. That lead can delegate complete implementation while retaining those responsibilities. An explicitly adopted overall coordinator has the separate coordination role below.
- Apply model selection to both the lead agent and delegated tasks. It does not require creating subagents or building a scheduling framework. Choose within the tools and delegation authority actually available.
- Follow explicit user model choices, applicable project overrides, and higher-priority instructions. The defaults below do not override them.

## Task start and execution routing

Establish the intended outcome, current slice, authoritative sources, effective Human authorization, and any existing division of responsibility. Reuse a clear assignment and valid evidence. A self-contained question or ordinary short task can be completed in the current chat; it does not require a new chat, Goal, workspace, or preliminary Astra stage. For work that needs organization, make the following choices before creating or dispatching the corresponding chat and before taking on the whole implementation. Revisit only choices affected by changed scope or material inputs.

When an overall-coordinator arrangement is explicitly adopted, the overall coordinator performs coordination only: task decomposition and dispatch, dependency and integration order, follow-up, arranging acceptance, and handoff. Assign business investigation, architecture, implementation, repairs, and substantive acceptance to the responsible task or domain leads. The coordinator can verify delivery bindings and evidence completeness and arrange business acceptance; it does not take business design or code implementation back from those owners. References below to a lead's business judgments, design, implementation, or substantive acceptance mean the responsible task or domain lead. This division does not require an extra coordination layer for ordinary tasks.

Select the responsible role and [model](#current-defaults) separately from the client, receiving service, execution mechanism, and execution host, applying [runtime limits](#runtime-limits) to the actual invocation. Use Astra High for complex investigation, architecture, and key acceptance judgments under the responsible task or domain lead. Once the design and slice are clear, use the effective Human division of work to hand the complete implementation, checks, and repairs to a separate Sol High chat when that arrangement is authorized. The Astra task or domain lead retains design and acceptance; ordinary implementation choices remain with Sol. Apply [escalation](#escalation) when implementation reveals a new business, authorization, transaction, or recovery judgment, continuing unaffected work while that lead resolves it.

Apply [local development](#local-development) when choosing the execution host and the [workspace rules](#workspaces) when choosing a new chat's binding. Each owner completes the assigned implementation, validation, signing, publication, and repairs from a suitable local workspace within effective authorization, using the project's normal Git, PR, and CI flow. Coordinate integration through identified Git revisions and their evidence. A change of role, model, or chat does not require copying the candidate or creating another checkout.

When organized implementation belongs to another project, route the slice to that project's responsible chat before writing source. Reuse a suitable existing owner. The recipient verifies the original grant, target project, checkout, candidate, writable scope, and acceptance conditions under [delegation and acceptance](#delegation-and-acceptance), then accepts the assignment. The originating chat retains coordination and its own project's work. A cross-project request does not transfer workspace ownership or authorize a competing writer. If the target owner or dispatch authority is missing, resolve that gap and continue independent authorized preparation.

When authorized to organize independent tasks, prefer visible, persistent chats. Use that route for complete independent implementation, PR delivery, follow-up repairs, or ongoing responsibility. Use authorized internal subagents for small, well-defined auxiliary work within the same task, returning results to the original owner; preserve the writable scope and single-writer requirements. The short-task rule above still allows self-contained work in the current chat. Choose the actual client and execution mechanism, including [Claude Code with DeepSeek](#claude-code-and-deepseek-delegation), by the complete result, acceptance needs, supported capabilities, and cost of splitting a cohesive responsibility. Apply the single [route preference](#route-model-preferences) before assigning execution, including complete implementation. A visible-chat request requires a visible chat; an internal subagent does not substitute for it. If the required native chat entry point is unavailable, report the specific gap and select a supported, authorized path; do not mask an independent-chat requirement with a subagent. This preference grants no chat-creation authority. Create chats only under effective Human authorization, reusing a covered batch grant without a new approval for each covered dispatch. Do not create a chat for every small step. Use the [delegation contract](#delegation-and-acceptance) for binding, original authorization, writer ownership, result delivery, and acceptance.

For design-draft creation and iteration, use the Codex model and effort in [current defaults](#current-defaults), honoring an explicit Human choice under [runtime limits](#runtime-limits). Apply the existing [design workflow](../skills/figma-design-workflow/SKILL.md) for editable results, critique, and pixel acceptance. Choose Figma when the task needs it; a local editable preview can satisfy a design task whose required output allows it. Claude Code with DeepSeek and the Design plugin are optional routes under effective Human choices, not prerequisites for producing a draft. Terra can assist with reference-image understanding and draft inspection; the lead retains product and interaction judgments and final acceptance. For browser or desktop interface operation, default to Terra under the model table and runtime limits. Prefer a suitable connector or API when available; a model preference does not require using a GUI.

Before execution, settle the applicable [chat and workspace binding](#workspaces) and whether an authorized [Goal](#goals) is appropriate. The recipient verifies the original requirements, design, execution binding, permitted operations, and acceptance conditions before dependent work. Proceed autonomously through the selected slice and its repairs under the existing [implementation rules](../AGENTS.md#implementation-slices). Route a ready result and the original owner's next authorized action promptly through the existing [acceptance and follow-up rules](#delegation-and-acceptance), including notifying or waking that owner when action is ready. Use those rules to distinguish actual dependencies or running operations from actionable work instead of leaving a ready task idle. Asynchronous observation, review, succession, and archival retain their existing owners below. A turn ending alone does not complete the responsibility.

## Local development

Run development on the physical development device selected by the Human: source edits, builds, debugging, and developer-run tests, including delegated commands and their child processes. The selection can cover the current Mac or an SSH-connected native Linux device such as `dev`. Without an effective selection of another device, keep the current verified development binding. In this policy, local execution means execution on that selected device; a Desktop window on the Mac does not make an SSH project's files or commands run on the Mac. Bind the actual host, repository, candidate, and tools under [workspaces](#workspaces). An existing candidate, installed tools, spare resources, or a visible chat alone does not select another device or authorize cloud development. Frontend, backend, design, and acceptance retain their assigned owners and separate workspaces where needed.

Use the project's supported native environment on the selected device. A VM or container is optional when project-supported and already authorized; neither is a prerequisite or an installed capability without actual evidence. Honor an explicit native-only project choice. Verify source compatibility, dependencies, and required checks on the actual host. Selecting Linux or installing Codex rules does not prove that a project or skill supports Linux. Keep macOS-only clients and native or pixel acceptance on a capable, authorized device, binding evidence to the same candidate. Installing or starting an environment retains its own operation authority and any existing pause; a device selection does not restart stopped Docker or provision a new VM.

Apply the shared [untrusted-input execution boundary](tool-contracts.md#不可信输入的执行) before running candidate-controlled code or dependencies. Local development does not require executing untrusted inputs on the native host. When suitable local isolation is unavailable, follow that contract's static preparation and existing isolated CI path while continuing independent trusted local work. CI remains validation rather than a replacement development host; a missing execution boundary blocks only the affected operation.

When the Human changes the development device, apply [running instructions](#running-instructions) before the next affected action. Identify the original candidate, WIP, writer, and in-flight or unknown operations, and establish the selected execution binding before continuing affected development. A later explicit selection supersedes an earlier location choice only within its effective scope. Old prompts, Goals, host assignments, and historical success do not restore a superseded location. Recovery, transfer, termination, and cleanup retain their existing authorization, ownership, and readback conditions; unknown state remains unknown. Use the normal Git delivery path for integration, preserving exact revisions and evidence. Continue independent authorized work while resolving the specific transition gap.

The local execution boundary preserves authorized model services, Git hosting and transport, formal review, project CI, and separately authorized deployment or service operations. Invoke selected model services through the authorized local client or connector. These services do not become development hosts, and their availability grants no new configuration, installation, publication, or remote-operation authority. Keep branch names such as `dev` and historical evidence in their actual scopes.

### Desktop capabilities

In Codex Desktop, use the current tool inventory, applicable skills, project commands, and verified local bindings to select an execution path. Prefer a suitable connected tool or API, then an appropriate existing local command or interface route. Use the available project, managed-worktree, chat, terminal, browser, desktop, and specialist capabilities when the task calls for them. Reuse unchanged availability and successful invocation evidence; do not start every task with a full capability audit or trial call.

Check the tool surface of the actual executor and host. A child chat, internal subagent, or delegated CLI does not automatically inherit the Desktop caller's browser, app connections, or chat-management tools. A shared account, version, or rules installation alone establishes neither shared sessions nor a reverse communication path. Multiple clients or transports can reach the same server; their different entry points do not isolate writers or workspaces. Verify the intended session and direction through the actual client; a host or thread identifier alone does not establish routing or access to an existing terminal session. Within effective collaboration authority, bind the affected stage to an executor with the required tools, preserving the candidate, writer ownership, and result path under [delegation and acceptance](#delegation-and-acceptance). Keep the coordinator's role and the responsible lead's acceptance duties; do not take back all execution or relocate development merely because one executor lacks a tool.

Resolve a real capability gap at the operation that needs it. Distinguish an exposed entry point from a working connection, installed project dependencies, and coverage of a required platform behavior. Use the [workspace dependency path](#workspaces) and existing [check rules](review.md#checks) for the affected gap. Preserve actual refusals and pauses, continue unaffected work, and report only the missing operation and responsible owner. Desktop availability does not prove a plugin is authenticated, a worktree contains ignored dependencies, or a local run covers Linux, OCI, or real-device behavior. Do not change personal settings or restart stopped tools just to complete a capability list.

Filter candidates by permitted receiving services and material, supported account and invocation arrangement, actual tools, input modalities, available usage, and task acceptance needs. Then choose a suitable model and effort. Model names alone do not establish these conditions. Prefer an adequate lower-cost execution option without transferring unresolved business judgments away from their owner. Do not run a comparison across models merely to select one or add a per-call billing approval.

DeepSeek is an optional route when the task and actual capabilities fit, with no fixed call share or quota target. This preference does not authorize model trials, new material recipients outside the task grant, or bypassing permissions.

The Codex table above owns defaults for Codex leads and chats. ## Escalation

- Move a Luna task to Sol High when it requires understanding a call chain or affects behavior in multiple places. Move it directly to Astra High when it involves key business or responsibility judgments.
- Move a Sol task to Astra High when it introduces a judgment about fact ownership, state rules, authorization order, transaction boundaries, or recovery responsibility. Resolve that judgment before extending implementation that depends on it.
- At Astra High, first gather the authoritative rules and trace the actual call chain. If rules still conflict, a major tradeoff remains unresolved, failures remain unexplained, or repairs of the same failure class repeatedly fail, identify the specific contradiction or evidence gap before moving to Extra High. Reconsider the underlying assumptions instead of repeating them with more reasoning. Return to the regular effort once the issue is clarified.
- Ask the Human for missing business decisions. Escalation neither supplies those decisions nor expands the [implementation slice](../AGENTS.md#implementation-slices) or [operation authority](../AGENTS.md#operation-boundaries).
- Within a selected external route, use the [route model preferences](#route-model-preferences) for the assigned result. A lighter execution model that encounters an unresolved responsibility or capability boundary returns that judgment to the lead; it does not silently change providers or take over acceptance. Raise only supported effort for a specific remaining uncertainty, choose a stronger suitable family when needed within authority, and return to ordinary execution after the issue is settled. Do not make maximum effort the default for all work.

## Runtime limits

Use only models, reasoning levels, and switching mechanisms the actual tool interface supports. Before creating, continuing, or changing a chat, select for the destination's assigned result and map the selected, authorized model and effort to the supported invocation parameters. A model choice for generating a design draft does not automatically cover a separate rule-maintenance or product-implementation chat. Inspect the interface's conditions for overriding defaults first. When an override requires an explicit Human model choice, identify that original choice and its scope. Reuse an explicitly adopted ongoing mapping that specifies the model for this role while its scope and the interface's conditions still apply; a tool requiring a choice in the current message retains that narrower condition. A general repository preference or an agent-authored role prompt does not establish an explicit Human choice. When the conditions are unmet, preserve the required default and report the difference rather than claiming the selected model was requested.

During the existing dispatch and reception checks, verify the emitted parameters and available supported readback. Distinguish the requested model and effort, client-saved chat settings, the active turn's model and effort, and server execution identity and effort. A success receipt containing only a chat ID establishes neither saved settings nor an active-turn switch. Report only the unverified layer as unknown; role text, model self-description, or a client selection does not prove server identity. Unknown server identity does not excuse omitting permitted parameters. A continuation request does not prove an in-progress turn changed unless the interface guarantees that behavior or trusted turn metadata verifies it. Reuse unchanged invocation and readback evidence under the existing [check rules](review.md#checks).

An explicitly adopted ongoing mapping binds the original Human instruction, covered project and outcome, assigned roles, and the retrievable table or revision actually selected. Reuse that selection within scope. A newer table, another project, or an agent-authored dispatch does not create a new Human model choice; follow later table updates only when the original choice explicitly covers them. A family preference without a resolved concrete identifier does not satisfy an interface that requires an explicit choice of a specific model. Editing a template does not retroactively adopt its new choices for existing tasks. Use the existing task context rather than a separate selection ledger.

Evaluate model and effort fields separately under the actual interface. A restriction on overriding the model does not itself forbid an effort override. When the default model is known and the interface permits a compatible selected effort, transmit that effort even if the model field must remain omitted. When compatibility is unknown, preserve the unsupported field as unset and explain only that gap; do not guess the default or silently inherit maximum effort as though it were the task choice. When valid existing authorization covers both fields, send both supported values without asking for the same choice again.

Use an available, authorized mechanism to take back or reassign an escalated task. If the main session cannot switch, or the selected model or effort is unavailable, report the limitation and any verified runtime identity. Request user action only when continuation actually depends on it. Otherwise continue the work supported by the available environment and existing authority, stating any fallback without silently replacing an explicit user choice. While a necessary decision or switch is pending, continue unaffected work.

Apply authorized per-call or per-chat model choices through supported parameters; distinguish those choices from persistent personal or global configuration changes, which retain their own authorization boundary. Do not automatically change persistent configuration or expand tool permissions to satisfy a model preference. Preserve tool refusals and higher-priority restrictions. Model selection does not grant access to a model, a service, or additional operations. Product controls are described in the [official OpenAI model documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/models); verify what the current environment actually exposes.

## Workspaces

Under [local development](#local-development), bind source work to the actual repository directory on the selected device, or its confirmed working directory when the work has no Git repository. Where the client exposes Project grouping, select the corresponding existing Project on that host and verify its repository or directory; a Project name or the client UI's host alone does not bind execution. A client without Project grouping can start directly in the correct directory. If no suitable project or repository exists and establishing one is necessary, ask the Human whether to establish it. Non-Git work does not require creating a Git repository.

Default a new chat that will edit files in a local Git working tree to one independent worktree, including when its only changes are versioned documents or acceptance evidence. Reuse a suitable worktree already provisioned for the chat. A clean main checkout alone does not satisfy this default. Necessary worktrees inside an existing repository are ordinary authorized preparation.

Default new chats for investigation, coordination, read-only review, or acceptance without worktree edits to Local: the existing project directory on the selected host. Local describes the workspace, independent of its current branch and the chat's permissions. Authorized GitHub operations and outputs kept outside the repository do not by themselves require an implementation worktree. If the assignment later adds worktree edits, reassess the binding under the continuation rule below before editing.

Continue an existing chat in its verified suitable workspace on the selected device, including an existing main-checkout binding; this default does not require migration on resumption. Verify that an existing SSH or other remote binding remains covered by the effective device selection under [local development](#local-development). Read-only work can inspect the existing candidate without owning another implementation worktree. Non-Git work uses its confirmed directory. An explicit Human workspace choice or a concrete client constraint can require a different arrangement on the selected device; identify the applicable exception and preserve actual refusals. When the default cannot be established, continue independent preparation and resolve the specific binding gap before dependent implementation. Inspect changes, their value and ownership, and active operations before using any workspace. Preserve useful WIP under its owner and isolate work when sharing would interfere. Remove disposable changes only under effective cleanup authorization after verifying their scope and recovery requirements; calling changes useless does not authorize discarding another task's work.

Limit each chat to one owned source-implementation worktree, whether managed or native. At the first implementation turn, on resumption, and before allocating another checkout, verify the chat's existing binding from its available attachments, actual Git worktree state, and task history. Count a worktree provisioned by the client before the first model turn as the chat's existing worktree. Reuse an existing binding; do not create successive worktrees for separate implementation purposes in the same chat or use a clone or copied implementation checkout to evade the limit. When a second independent implementation worktree is necessary, assign it to a separately authorized chat and verify that chat's actual binding before work starts. Restore the same worktree through its supported recovery path when needed. Restoring that binding does not allocate a second implementation workspace. Disposable Git fixtures created and cleaned entirely by a foreground test do not count toward this limit; do not use that exception for implementation checkouts. If the required chat or workspace mechanism is unavailable, report the specific gap and continue independent preparation rather than creating an unowned second worktree.

Design, implementation, and acceptance can use the same suitable checkout in stages. An independent acceptor may inspect the author's stable worktree or preview with authorized access while keeping their own primary workspace and attachment. Read-only access does not transfer ownership. Before transferring a write stage, verify the actual candidate and WIP, account for in-flight or unknown operations under their existing handles and owners, and obtain the receiving writer's acceptance. The outgoing writer stops writing before the recipient starts. Unchanged ownership needs no repeated handoff. Reuse valid evidence under the [check rules](review.md#checks); a role or chat change alone does not require reconstructing the candidate or rerunning checks. Isolate when concurrent writes, useful unrelated WIP, or actual client constraints prevent safe sharing.

When local validation lacks a dependency, identify the uncovered checks and the project's supported local preparation or already authorized CI path. Keep implementation and delivery with their assigned owners. Bind any separate validation to the actual candidate and obtain evidence of the commands and behavior it covers; a workflow file alone proves no run. Explain an unresolved platform or resource gap while continuing independent local work under [local development](#local-development). Preserve required integrated coverage, original operation handles, unknown outcomes, and permission boundaries. Normal Git fetch and publication, actual source inspection, formal review, and necessary recovery preservation remain available within effective authorization; they do not require a new approval for each transfer.

For the new-chat default or necessary isolation, prefer the client's managed worktree mechanism for the intended repository and verified baseline. Check the actual client, repository, and session operation. If a creation tool reserves its Worktree option for an explicit Human choice, omit that option unless the choice is covered. Where supported, start in Local and establish the writer's independent binding before any repository write. A policy default does not change a client option or grant permission. Shared access does not make another chat's checkout attachable. Verify the supported attachment and lifecycle operations before relying on a transfer. An absent MCP tool alone does not establish lack of client support. When the managed mechanism is actually unavailable, state the concrete reason before using a native Git worktree within the existing preparation authority. Preserve actual refusals under the [operation boundaries](../AGENTS.md#operation-boundaries).

Apply the single [execution context contract](tool-contracts.md#执行上下文) before editing or validating, including the actual chat directory, tool target, candidate, and applicable Project grouping. For a managed worktree, wait for creation and registration and read back the attachment. For a native Git worktree, verify its actual Git worktree binding without claiming a managed attachment. A requested mode, ordinary clone, success receipt, or matching HEAD alone does not prove the binding. If necessary isolation remains unavailable, identify that concrete gap and continue independent preparation; do not create a workspace in the wrong repository or change client configuration to simulate support. Preserve existing WIP and active operations under their current owner. Move existing work only at a safe handoff, with its inputs and outstanding operations accounted for and the new binding verified.

Keep acceptance originals and required ignored recovery material outside a disposable checkout, or copy and verify them there before retirement. Follow the [completion and archival requirements](#task-completion-and-chat-archival) before retiring the worktree or archiving its chat. The [official worktree documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/environments/git-worktrees#worktree-cleanup) describes automatic cleanup, including on chat archival and under the configured retention limit. Inspect actual results; do not assume that keeping a chat retains its checkout indefinitely. This policy does not authorize changing those settings.

## Task temporary directories

Keep disposable command output under one private allocation with a traceable task or chat owner and a stated purpose. Reuse that allocation for the same foreground operation instead of scattering scratch directories. Read the [temporary-directory tool contract](tool-contracts.md#task-temp). Use tool `task-temp` for foreground commands that support its scratch environment and await all descendants. Keep Git worktrees under the existing workspace lifecycle, and keep shared caches with their capability owner. For tools with their own temporary-resource lifecycle, use their supported cleanup and verify its result rather than nesting a second owner around them.

Keep acceptance originals, recovery snapshots, and diagnostics that must survive outside disposable scratch before cleanup. A known foreground exit permits the wrapper to clean only its newly created allocation under the command's effective preparation and cleanup authority. An interruption, unknown external operation, background process, or ownership change requires owner verification; ending a wait or observing an absent PID does not establish termination. Preserve retained allocation paths and the specific remaining condition in the existing task result, without a separate ledger.

At task closeout, account for temporary resources created by that task alongside the existing worktree checks: verify removal, or state the owner, retention reason, and next condition for cleanup. Use the read-only scan to locate leftovers after interrupted execution. Existing unmarked directories remain unknown; age, naming, or an idle chat does not authorize deletion. Do not retrofit ownership markers onto historical directories to make them eligible for automatic removal. Periodic deletion and historical cleanup require their own effective scope and authority.

Inspect the command's cleanup result when it exits, before treating its temporary resources as settled. On resumption after interruption, resolve the recorded allocation, original operation, and retained condition before reusing or replacing the scratch directory. Inspect the known allocation first; use a bounded read-only scan only when its location is missing. If the temporary-directory tool is unavailable, report the missing delivery and use an existing project-owned lifecycle with equivalent ownership and cleanup when available. Otherwise continue work that does not depend on allocating scratch; do not silently scatter unowned directories or install personal configuration.

## Goals

Create a Goal only when sustained pursuit is appropriate and the Human or applicable higher-priority instructions explicitly authorize Goal creation for that outcome and chat. A complex task, a new chat, an assignment to keep working, or another chat's Goal alone supplies no creation authority. Without it, continue the authorized task normally. A Goal records the intended outcome, acceptance criteria, scope, key constraints, exclusions, and completion conditions; keep progress, logs, operation handles, and evidence in existing task material. A Goal does not grant publication, merge, deployment, or additional scope.

During formal succession for the same business objective, verify the original explicit Goal-creation authorization, its effective coverage of the successor chat or collaborating role, and the successor's actual Goal state. When that authority still covers the successor and `get_goal` returns `null`, reuse it to create the full Goal without asking for the same approval again. Authorization limited to the original chat, an uncovered role, or a different objective does not authorize creation in the successor; continue independent authorized work normally.

Construct the successor Goal from the original Goal and latest effective instructions. Full means preserving the intended outcome, acceptance, scope, effective authorization and constraints, exclusions, completion conditions, repairs, and remaining responsibilities; it does not require copying the original text verbatim. Record the scope and conditions of any effective continuing authorization, retain its retrievable source, and verify that it still applies before reusing it. Reconcile each omission against the sources. Keep progress, completed events, identifiers, logs, handles, and detailed evidence out of the Goal when they do not define a continuing responsibility or constraint; retain necessary material under its existing privacy and retention boundaries. Carry forward the responsibility and owner for unresolved effects or operations. Do not discard a fact merely because it is old or appears in a one-time instruction when it still limits or authorizes work.

Create the chat and Goal as separate operations through the [supported Goal interfaces](#goal-tool-interfaces), then read back the complete effective Goal and compare it with the authorized source. A creation receipt or summary does not establish the effective text. Preserve an existing unfinished Goal and its owner; do not mark it complete to make room for another one.

When new instructions affect the objective or a constraint, reconcile the latest effective instructions with the full outcome, acceptance, exclusions, repairs, and remaining responsibility. Treat superseded implementation choices as retired, and preserve responsibility for their historical effects and unresolved operations. Use a supported amendment or safe continuation or handoff under existing authorization; preserve the interface limitation when text or status cannot be changed. Do not recreate a narrower objective, modify a status database, or claim completion to bypass that limitation. A paused publication stage does not end independent local work that remains authorized. Apply [automatic-review policy succession](#automatic-review-policy-succession) when an effective task grant covers that workflow. Follow [continuity](#continuity-and-evidence) and [completion](#task-completion-and-chat-archival) for actual responsibility transfer and termination.

Assess a proposed new outcome or improvement against the original requirements, latest instructions, and effective continuing authorization. Correct omissions and defects within the authorized outcome; select an independent outcome under the [continuing delivery boundary](../AGENTS.md#continuing-delivery). A suggestion alone grants no scope or operation authority. When the existing Goal text cannot be amended, preserve it and make the effective instructions, their source, and the resulting remaining responsibilities retrievable in existing task material. The owner and successor reconcile those materials with the Goal before dependent work and at handoff; do not claim the stored text has changed.

### Goal tool interfaces

Check the actual interface before calling it. Currently, `create_thread` has no Goal parameter. Verify chat creation and its actual target before using `create_goal` in the chat covered by explicit creation authorization. Put the full objective in `objective`; set `token_budget` only when explicitly requested. An unfinished Goal prevents creating another one. Use `get_goal` to read back the complete effective text.

Currently, `update_goal` changes status only; it cannot amend the objective or restore `active`. Set `paused` only at the Human's explicit request, report the returned status, and stop Goal work. Set `complete` only when the objective is achieved and no required work remains; for a budgeted Goal, report the final token usage returned by the tool. Set `blocked` only after the same blocker has recurred for at least three consecutive Goal turns, including the initial turn, and no meaningful progress remains possible. Resuming a blocked Goal starts a fresh blocked audit. Difficulty, slow progress, or incomplete work alone does not satisfy that condition. Resumption and budget or usage limits remain under Human or system control. Recheck the applicable constraints when the interface changes.

## Automatic-review policy succession

When the Human authorizes this workflow for a project and outcome, the owner identifies permission gaps while work continues. Bind each gap to the requested action, target, effective Human authorization and source, and actual technical or review constraint. If relevant callers or failure handlers are unavailable, narrow the assignment and state the gap; do not infer a defect from omitted code. Read-only assignments remain read-only. Execution authority does not add publication, merge, deployment, credential, or permission changes; perform those only when separately covered by effective authorization.

### Lead succession

When accumulated context impairs the work, prepare a successor chat at an appropriate task boundary using an available, authorized mechanism. Do not assume automatic transfer of runtime state or permissions. A fork retains conversation history; it does not clear context. See the [official OpenAI guidance on chat context](https://developers.openai.com/blog/mastering-codex-remote-for-engineering).

For a lead coordinating continuing work across task chats, check succession readiness at 90 minutes and normally complete the handoff between 90 and 120 minutes, with a 120-minute upper limit. Measure elapsed wall time from the initial assumption of coordination or the confirmed takeover, using the existing task record. A turn ending, a readiness check, or context compaction does not restart that clock. Begin preparing the handoff material 10–15 minutes before the planned handoff. These timings govern continuing coordination; they do not impose scheduled chat replacement, new roles, approvals, or model rounds on ordinary bounded tasks.

At the readiness check, verify that the lead can identify the current owners, candidates, and next actions using valid existing evidence. Bring the handoff forward as soon as the lead confuses owners or candidates, repeatedly rechecks accepted evidence, or frequently rereads old records just to determine the next action. Start preparation immediately when such impairment appears; do not wait for the scheduled check or spend a minimum preparation interval before transferring responsibility.

Choose a handoff boundary that preserves active work without waiting for every task, CI run, or review to finish. Being busy does not extend the upper limit. At that limit, keep new dispatch paused if transfer is still unconfirmed, report the concrete succession gap, and continue only the reconciliation needed to resolve it. Elapsed time neither transfers responsibility nor completes the goal; resume dispatch only after the confirmation below.

At the handoff boundary, the outgoing lead pauses new dispatch, including replacement write tasks. Keep active tasks under their known ownership during that pause. Use the existing task record to identify the current goal and slice, host and actual workspace, branch, baseline and candidate including WIP, active task IDs and writer ownership, effective authorization and its original source, evidence, and unresolved work. Preserve original operation handles and their observers rather than restarting operations or creating competing writers or observers. Make the full current Goal available for reconciliation under [Goals](#goals), and include the actual recovery-reminder target and status. Do not create a separate permanent handoff log.

The successor reads back and verifies those materials, resolves dependent gaps, and explicitly accepts responsibility. Apply [Goals](#goals) to the original creation authority, the successor's actual Goal state, and the required full-text reconciliation and readback. The outgoing lead confirms the handoff and stops dispatching before the successor starts assigning work. Keep the dispatch pause until that confirmation so both leads cannot assign competing write tasks. If confirmation is missing, report succession as pending and resolve the ownership gap before dispatch resumes.

Transfer existing authorized heartbeat or recovery reminders to the accepted successor through the supported mechanism and read back the effective target and conditions. Preserve the original notification and stopping conditions, prevent duplicate recovery dispatch, and report an unavailable transfer as a follow-up gap rather than claiming monitoring continues. This does not authorize new reminders or configuration changes. Evaluate the outgoing chat under the existing [completion and archival rules](#task-completion-and-chat-archival), preserving actual acceptance, remaining responsibility, runtime status, and archive readback.

Claim successful succession only after the actual responsibility transfer is confirmed. Static scenarios and ordinary context compaction or recovery do not establish that a new lead has taken over.
