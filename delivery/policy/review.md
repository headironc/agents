# Validation and review

## Checks

- Read-only review permits running relevant tests and checks, including writing disposable build output, caches, logs, and fixtures in designated local locations. Preserve the reviewed source, tracked snapshots, dependencies, persistent configuration, and unrelated work. Inspect unfamiliar test entrypoints for effects before running them; updating snapshots or running an automatic fix changes the reviewed material. Operations on real services or business data remain subject to their own authorization. Grant the specific validation commands and required output locations rather than denying execution categorically or granting unrestricted access. When the environment blocks a check, report it as not run and distinguish any existing author-run evidence from reviewer-run evidence.
- Run the project's required checks and tests that cover the changed behavior. Before an expensive full acceptance run, check the environment, type, and fixture critical path: temporary paths, required permissions, and the selected actor where relevant. Reuse effective incremental results for unchanged inputs and rerun only the affected checks, unless a required full gate, a shared effect, or unresolved risk requires more. A wording change in a report does not mechanically invalidate unrelated verification.
- Do not reduce required coverage to save time. Business behavior, authorization, concurrency, failures, recovery, cleanup, real interaction, formal review or admission, and any required queue combination keep their own checks. Follow the project's [adopted merge workflow](#merge-admission).
- Inspect the final diff for correctness and unrelated changes. Do not add tests that merely repeat implementation or enforce obsolete names.
- Ordinary reversible internal changes do not require a separate design or implementation reviewer by default. Follow an explicit project requirement or Human request for review.
- Before each authorized push, run the relevant required checks and inspect the final diff. Reuse passing results for unchanged inputs. Opening or updating a PR does not require a separate pre-push review; fixes to review feedback may be pushed after those checks. Publication still requires its authorization.

## Review provider selection

Prefer Cubic when its applicable subscription or public allowance covers the repository and review. Bind coverage to the actual owner, account, and usage pool; visibility alone does not establish coverage. The Human's Cubic subscription covers personal repositories under `headironc`, including private repositories. That subscription does not cover private organization repositories, which use Codex. A public repository may use Cubic's public allowance when its actual eligibility and charging pool are confirmed; do not assume that it uses the personal subscription or an unlimited allowance.

Within authorization to complete the required review, automatically use Codex when Cubic is confirmed outside the applicable coverage or the applicable pool, including its monthly allowance, is exhausted. For GitHub PRs, complete the missing review through the [supported PR trigger](../skills/gh/references/operations.md#automatic-review-and-feedback): issue the request, observe completion, and consume the result. Do not stop at recommending the switch or ask again for the same scoped authorization. Use a different route for a GitHub PR only when the Human explicitly chooses it or the adopted project contract specifies it. Establish the reason Cubic cannot review from its supported account and request evidence. A near-limit notice, timeout, HTTP 429, missing comment, billing or seat error, or an elapsed reset time alone proves neither quota exhaustion nor recovery. Handle incomplete or stalled requests under [missing automatic review](#missing-automatic-review) and [stalled review recovery](#stalled-review-recovery), preserving their request binding and unknown-write limits. Do not turn an unresolved provider failure into repeated requests to either provider.

Before requesting the fallback, establish the supported Codex invocation identity, charged account and usage pool, and trigger permissions. Check available usage evidence and follow effective Human limits and adopted project constraints, including known exhausted usage. Do not require an unsupported endpoint for exact remaining quota for ordinary fallback. Require an account-wide reserve guarantee only when the Human or adopted project explicitly requires it. In that case, verify the provider's actual enforcement, including shared-pool consumption and automatic or manual triggers outside the selected entry point. Request counts, a repository concurrency limit, or a percentage snapshot alone do not prove that reserve. If the required guarantee is unavailable, hold the dependent request and obtain the Human's decision on the specific gap or reduced guarantee. Without an adopted reserve requirement, the absence of a platform hard lock does not block the ordinary authorized fallback. Do not invent budget thresholds, purchase credits, change accounts, alter settings, or replace the requested automatic fallback with a disabled default as though the Human had chosen it.

Reuse valid current-candidate coverage and existing requests before selecting another provider. Bind a new request to the exact repository, PR, head, target baseline, and missing review kinds; account for kinds coupled by one trigger. Observe a known request through its existing handle and resolve an unknown write before another request. A provider switch preserves prior findings and their dispositions. Confirm Cubic recovery for subsequent uncovered work rather than rerunning a candidate that already has valid coverage.

Provider selection does not change the [coverage requirements](#review-coverage) or the project's [adopted merge workflow](#merge-admission). The project owns any provider adapter and its acceptance evidence, including code and security coverage, trusted result identity, candidate and baseline binding, completion, findings, and invalidation. A Cubic result cannot satisfy a Codex-only gate without an authorized and verified contract change. Keep rule publication, provider configuration, actual review, and adopted platform enforcement as separate outcomes.

## Asynchronous observation

At the start of observation, identify every result surface that can affect the next action, including CI status, review findings and dispositions, review completion, and final admission where applicable. Assign one responsible observer to each surface and bind the original operation handle, candidate, and completion conditions. Prefer supported native CLI or tool waits for the surfaces they cover. Observe uncovered findings and other required surfaces concurrently; do not wait for CI to finish before starting review observation. Keep waits in their running processes or tools and resume their existing sessions, without duplicate observers or repeated reads of unchanged logs. One owner can use complementary waits; this does not require a single process or a fixed topology. Coordinators consume compact owner updates with supported cursors instead of observing the same underlying target again.

When no suitable native wait covers a required result surface, use a minimal, bounded, read-only observer for the current task. Bind its actual process or tool session, target, deadline, and stop conditions. Confirm an initial read succeeded before claiming observation is active. Keep only the fields that can change the next action, while preserving identities needed to locate the original evidence. Compare semantic state within the observer and remain silent while it is unchanged. Timestamps, repeated log text, or replacement check IDs with unchanged meaning alone do not justify returning to the model. Preserve changes that affect binding, evidence validity, findings, or admission; normalization must not hide them. This fallback does not require a reusable polling framework.

Return to the model for completion, a new finding or failure, candidate or relevant baseline drift, queue removal, a material admission change, a real read error or timeout, or a needed decision. Read the affected evidence then. A partial or failed read leaves the current state unknown; it is neither an empty result nor proof that the prior state remains current. If a tool yields without such an event, continue its existing wait without another analysis or unchanged-status report. Keep required user communication, but do not use model turns as a polling timer.

Distinguish an observation window ending, an observer process exiting, one workflow phase finishing, and the original operation reaching its completion conditions. When a native check watcher finishes but an identified review or gate remains pending, retain observation of that remaining surface without rerunning completed checks. A missing route, an unidentifiable run, or a completed run with missing evidence calls for a specific gap report or recovery under its contract, not indefinite polling. Before replacing an observer, establish its terminal state so that ownership remains singular. Observation failure or expiry does not authorize restarting, retrying, or retriggering the underlying operation; apply the existing unknown-write and recovery rules.

Choose observation intervals and windows from supported tool behavior, service limits, and the response needed by the task. Report the actual latency and API-cost limits when relevant; neither zero token use nor immediate detection is guaranteed. Preserve a Human-specified total wait limit across rounds and handoffs. An agent-selected window can continue under the existing authorization when the same target is still pending, without a fixed round count or repeated permission request. Authorized scheduled checks recover interrupted follow-up; they do not replace active observation of the critical path.

### Missing automatic review

When a required automatic review has no usable result, the PR owner promptly checks the actual review route instead of indefinitely extending observation. Bind the repository, PR, current head, provider, required review kinds, original triggering event and applicable configuration. Read the provider's available run, request and result surfaces. An empty public summary or enabled setting alone does not establish whether a review ran. Follow a progressing run through its original handle, consume completed valid coverage, or investigate a completed run's missing return evidence before requesting another review. Apply [stalled review recovery](#stalled-review-recovery) when an original run remains running abnormally long without available progress. Reuse an applicable verified provider contract; a new PR alone does not require rediscovering it.

Human authorization to complete the required review within a workflow covers the necessary supported first manual request when the automatic route supplies no usable run or result, and supported recovery under the conditions below. Reuse that authorization across covered PRs without asking again for the same operation. Authorization limited to opening a PR and its automatic event does not cover manual requests. A one-time instruction to trigger a specific PR remains limited to that request; neither form authorizes arbitrary additional reviews, periodic retriggering, configuration changes or bypassing a refusal.

Before a first manual request, confirm the provider supports the manual mechanism and check its published restrictions on duplicate requests, replacement and coexistence. Re-read the exact head, available results and request records immediately before writing. Identify whether an earlier manual request was made and resolve any unknown write outcome first. When the provider hides internal runs, use the evidence it exposes and state the internal uncertainty. Unavailable internal liveness or coexistence proof is not a prerequisite for the first bounded recovery request under [stalled review recovery](#stalled-review-recovery). Preserve an actual provider restriction or refusal; report a specific unavailable mechanism or unmet published condition and stop only dependent work.

Request only the missing coverage through that supported mechanism and retain the request receipt and resulting run or result binding. Account for the review kinds one request starts under the current configuration; a request that starts both code and security review does not need a second request for security. Read back an unknown write on the same target without resending it. Continue observation of the confirmed request under the existing observation rules and preserve unaffected results. A further request requires a fresh recovery assessment and effective authorization; an elapsed observation window alone supplies neither. Missing authorization, unsupported recovery or an actual access refusal remains a concrete dependency, not permission to change settings or evade the refusal. Review recovery still follows [merge admission](#merge-admission).

### Stalled review recovery

When a required review appears abnormally stalled without a result, the PR owner investigates the review instead of only extending observation. Bind the exact PR, head, provider, review kind and run or round identity using the available trigger, time and summary when no internal run ID is exposed. Check elapsed time, the last meaningful event and the provider's available timing, progress and recovery evidence. An observation timeout or a still-running status alone does not establish a stalled review; there is no global minute threshold. An original automatic review that remains running abnormally long, with no meaningful progress on the available surfaces after a bounded investigation, calls for a first manual recovery request. Explain the abnormal wait using the task's observed history and any available provider timing; this is a recovery judgment, not proof that the hidden run has stopped. Do not keep extending waits or seeking unavailable internal liveness or coexistence proof. Immediately before recovery, read the current result surfaces and request records to check for completion or an existing replacement round. A new round on the same head changes the evidence binding even if its status is unchanged. Consume an existing valid result or follow the current round without launching another.

When that evidence supports recovery and effective Human authorization covers it, the original PR owner proactively sends one bounded request for the missing review through the provider's supported manual mechanism. Reuse effective workflow or standing recovery authorization without asking again for the same scope; retain narrower one-time limits. Respect published replacement, coexistence and attempt restrictions, but do not turn absent internal evidence or undocumented coexistence behavior into an additional approval requirement. Preserve the original run and findings, the recovery reason, the new request receipt and the confirmed run binding. Read back the request on the same target; if its outcome remains unknown, stop dependent writes without resending. Continue one observer per required surface on the confirmed current round, retaining unaffected CI and other review results and accounting for review kinds coupled by the mechanism. A further stall requires a fresh assessment, not periodic retriggering. If authorization, the supported manual mechanism or host access is missing, report the specific dependency and continue independent work. This rule grants no cancellation or configuration authority, bypass of a refusal, or change to the project's adopted merge requirements.

## Merge admission

- The repository owns its adopted merge workflow. Establish that contract from the target-branch baseline or independently trusted configuration before applying it to the candidate. A candidate cannot establish, lower, or broaden its own acceptance conditions or trust. Review and authorize governance changes under the established process before adopting them; changed source alone does not prove that platform configuration changed.
- Every adopted workflow retains applicable checks, formal code and security review of the current candidate and relevant target baseline, finding disposition, required discussions and approvals, explicit operation authority, actual platform protection, and result readback. The project declares which conditions the platform enforces, which the review process handles, and the accepted limitations. It may adopt native CI with separate review or a stronger enforced workflow; this policy does not require a custom controller, dedicated freshness check, aggregate admission signal, or merge queue by default.
- During workflow design and configuration acceptance, verify the results and producers the project claims to enforce. A controller can finish successfully while admission is pending or denied. Names, output text, and embedded identifiers do not establish platform isolation. Test the lifecycle and invalidation boundaries claimed by the adopted workflow, including creation, close/reopen, candidate reuse, or queue reentry where applicable; report unverified boundaries and accepted limitations in the project contract. These checks belong to governance work, not routine merge execution.
- Make repeated notifications idempotent when the bound candidate, queue entry where applicable, and required evidence remain valid. Preserve valid success and approvals instead of withdrawing them merely to announce reevaluation. Changed inputs or unavailable evidence invalidate the affected conditions under the project contract. The project owns review-baseline and integration-baseline semantics; routine merge agents do not recompute them.
- State trusted configuration assumptions and who maintains them. Authorized configuration owners verify them during enablement, changes, and explicit audits. A field omitted because the reader lacks permission is unknown, not an empty restriction or bypass list. Do not expand a gate's administration permissions solely to observe such a prerequisite, or claim that a check prevents authorized administrators from changing protection. Keep source publication, configuration activation, and actual acceptance as separate evidence.
- For an adopted native workflow, bind the exact repository, PR or other integration object, candidate, and target. Verify its applicable check and review evidence, dispositions, required discussions and approvals, and actual platform state under this contract. Reuse valid evidence and establish affected coverage after changes. The project's declared review process supplies its review conditions; an aggregate admission signal is required only when the project adopted one.
- For an adopted stronger workflow, consume its declared final admission state for that exact object, candidate, and target. Do not reconstruct it by auditing approval identities, baseline bindings, freshness, review threads, timelines, or protection internals. A green workflow or an approval alone is a final admission signal only when the established contract and platform enforcement make it one. An explicit evidence audit can diagnose a gap but cannot replace the required final state.
- Hold the dependent merge when required evidence is missing, stale, unreadable, incomplete, or contradictory, or when the actual platform refuses the operation. Missing enforcement or a gate unable to enforce conditions explicitly required by the adopted project contract is a governance gap. Report the specific gap for authorized repair; do not replace required enforcement with manual evidence reconstruction, another approver, or a temporary agent verdict. Native review under an adopted ordinary workflow is that workflow's established process, not a fallback for a stronger one.
- Authorized direct integration and stack layers follow the same adopted requirements and their own operation permissions. Without a PR, bind the target ref, expected current target revision, proposed resulting revision, and controlled integration operation. Consume a final enforced state when the project requires it; do not invent a PR or controller merely to perform the authorized operation.
- Reviewers and maintainers handle findings and their reasons during review. A machine result proves only the conditions it actually checks. Thread resolution, summary flags, and an approval do not prove semantic disposition or that the complete original report was assessed. Keep reasons with the original finding or source-report discussion; do not build a second disposition ledger or a general natural-language parser to simulate that judgment.
- Once the adopted conditions are satisfied, the platform permits the bound action, and explicit merge authority is present, execute the project's supported merge operation and read back the result. Use ordinary merge or the target branch's required queue flow. Keep pending queue or auto-merge acceptance distinct from a completed merge. Diagnose an unexpected queue removal or failed admission before any separately authorized resubmission. Never bypass protection or infer permission to retry from an unknown write outcome. Confirmed PR merge readback continues into [post-merge cleanup](../skills/gh/references/operations.md#post-merge-cleanup) before the workflow's final report.
- A known unresolved defect or contradictory evidence remains actionable even when a status is green. Stop the dependent merge and return the issue to review or governance repair. After resolution, satisfy the adopted workflow and recheck affected evidence; the investigation itself does not grant release permission.

## Review coverage

These rules govern review work, finding repair, and explicitly requested evidence audits. They define the review evidence used by the project's adopted merge workflow. Direct evidence verification in a native workflow does not replace a final enforced state required by a stronger one.

- Establish review coverage of the latest candidate against its intended target branch and baseline. An existing PR review establishes coverage when its actual inputs or documented input contract, exact revisions, findings, and limitations support it; a green review job alone does not. Reuse valid coverage. After a candidate or baseline change, establish coverage for the changed inputs while retaining unaffected evidence.
- Use trusted gate evidence only for conditions its established contract actually verifies. Identify uncovered conditions and known contradictions in the review or audit result. Evidence gathering follows the [adopted merge workflow](#merge-admission), including any explicitly required enforcement.

- Before relying directly on a repository-hosted review integration, identify its actual entry point and trigger for the named consumer, then read back its runtime state for the exact PR and head. An enabled workflow, installed app, configured check, or green CI job alone does not prove that the reviewer ran or covered the current head. A disabled or absent route is unavailable; use an available PR review or the Human-designated reviewer instead of preserving an obsolete requirement because its YAML remains.
- Judge a review's coverage from its actual inputs or documented input contract, the baseline and head it bound, any available tool record, and the reviewer's statements of scope and limits. Distinguish three coverage states: the report does not mention a file; no verifiable evidence shows the file was covered; and evidence shows it was not reviewed. A report that does not list files one by one, or a reviewer that ran no `git diff`, does not by itself show a file went unread; a workflow prompt or a green status does not by itself show that any particular file was read. When evidence is insufficient, state the exact gap rather than claiming the file was not reviewed.
- Read the selected reviewer tool's contract before invoking it. A route is unavailable when its reviewer is absent, its run cannot produce a review, or its range cannot be bound to the candidate. A recipe route requires evidence that the native recipe ran and a final report whose findings, locations, and reasons can be identified, or an explicit no-findings result. Record a deviation from the recipe's output shape without discarding usable findings. Require the declared machine-readable shape when an automated consumer depends on it. A free-form answer without recipe execution does not substitute for a recipe review. A route without a recipe must report findings grounded in changed lines and requirements or concrete defects, explicitly report that it found none in the bound scope, or satisfy the hosted completion rule below. Disclose failed attempts. If no route supplies valid coverage, hold the merge, not an otherwise authorized PR update.
- A hosted review may report a no-findings outcome through its own completion summary instead of a formal GitHub review or file-by-file transcript. Accept that bounded outcome when the provider's documented contract identifies the reviewed material, the verified review provider published the summary for its identified run, the summary binds the exact PR and current head, the reviewed target-branch baseline is verifiable, the trigger and final status are confirmed, and that run's published review surfaces contain no findings or failures. Track findings from earlier runs and their dispositions separately. If the target baseline changes after the review, obtain coverage for the new baseline before merging. Record the provider's scope and unknown per-file reads. An absent completion summary, an isolated reaction, a completed CI check, or a summary for an older head does not establish a no-findings outcome.

- Reuse a reviewer input contract already verified in the session or recorded with its source in the project's review guidance when it still applies to the selected provider and review route. A new PR or merge request alone does not require another official-documentation lookup. Recheck the contract when the provider or route changes, available evidence contradicts it, or a specific coverage question remains unanswered. Direct verification still binds each PR's actual run, revisions, and findings.
- Once the applicable coverage and finding-disposition requirements are satisfied, stop collecting review evidence. Report review completion and continue through the project's adopted merge workflow. Do not seek another report, official-documentation lookup, per-file transcript, or review run merely for additional reassurance. Reopen review verification only for a changed candidate or baseline, a new finding or failure, contradictory evidence, or a specific unmet requirement; identify that reason and check only the affected evidence.

## Findings and disposition

- Classify findings by present impact, not by the existence of a comment or a possible future edit. A blocking finding must relate to the bound candidate or behavior affected by that candidate; report unrelated pre-existing defects separately without making them merge conditions. Support an out-of-scope classification with verified change or call-chain evidence. Keep a verified defect pending while that evidence is missing; resolve its candidate relevance before treating the review as complete. A blocking finding identifies a current defect with its trigger and wrong outcome, or a violated requirement with its source. An uncovered safety or acceptance requirement blocks when existing evidence is insufficient. State the requirement and evidence gap; an incomplete report requires clarification or verification, not automatic downgrading. A hypothetical mutation that existing tests would miss is not by itself a current implementation defect. Classify useful regression hardening as non-blocking when current requirements are satisfied. Omit personal style preferences and speculative extensions. Record the reason for a non-blocking classification. When changing a finding's severity, retain the original classification and record the new classification, supporting evidence, and reason alongside its disposition.
- Review the relevant execution chain before publishing findings: entry conditions, input and identity binding, authorization, effects, outputs, and failure handling. Consolidate related omissions into actionable findings without hiding distinct defects. When fixing a finding, inspect adjacent cases of the same failure class within the authorized scope. Follow-up review focuses on the repair and affected behavior while retaining prior coverage of unchanged material. Report newly discovered valid findings, identifying them as repair-induced, previously missed, or outside the bound scope where the evidence supports that distinction; disclose uncertainty rather than inventing provenance.
- During review, resolve blocking findings and record a disposition for non-blocking suggestions: implemented, deferred with a reason, or declined with evidence. Keep the reason in the original finding thread or source-report discussion and reuse unchanged dispositions. Deferral does not claim the suggested change was made. Respect required thread resolution; resolving a thread records its disposition, not necessarily a code change. Valid current-candidate coverage, resolved candidate relevance for pending defects, no unresolved blocker, and recorded non-blocking dispositions complete the review requirement, even when suggestions remain unimplemented. Review completion satisfies only the review portion of the adopted workflow; operation authority and the remaining merge conditions still apply. Do not launch repeated reviews of unchanged material without new evidence. Do not require zero suggestions. Separately authorized fixes may proceed through the normal PR review cycle; seek a new decision when findings change the authorized scope, not merely because a round counter reached three.
- Before implementing a change to credentials, access control, destructive data migration, or hard-to-reverse external effects, explain the proposed boundary, failure handling, and validation to the Human and obtain approval covering that design. Beyond the merge review above, an additional design or implementation review is required only when the Human or project explicitly requires it.

## Review provenance

- Treat a review as evidence about a named candidate, not as proof that the judgment is independent or the candidate is correct. Prefer a reviewer who did not produce the material and can examine its requirements and authoritative sources without the author's conclusions; another model family may reduce shared blind spots. These choices reduce some correlations but do not certify the review. Honor an explicitly selected reviewer. A self-check remains useful validation; label it as a self-check. A self-check alone does not satisfy the merge review requirement. Do not require author-instance records or infer reviewer separation from model names, session trailers, subagent topology, or a cleared context.
- When the Human requests review without inherited context, start a reviewer agent with conversation inheritance disabled. Pass only the bound scope and revisions, review directions, safety constraints, and authoritative source locations, without the author's conclusions. If the available tool cannot provide that isolation, report the limitation instead of claiming an isolated review. Context isolation does not establish a different model family or independent judgment.
- When verifying review evidence directly, preserve the reviewer's report as produced and assess its findings rather than accepting only an author's summary. Record the reviewer and model when known, who selected the route, the bound baseline and candidate, the actual inputs and uncovered material, whether the scope or conclusions were supplied by the author, and whether the reviewer checked requirements or only internal consistency. Disclose known authorship or context overlap, model-family overlap, unknowns, failed attempts, and every run on the same candidate. Missing provenance limits what can be claimed about the reviewer; it does not invalidate an otherwise usable finding or establish that the reviewer shared the author's context. An explicit no-findings result establishes only that this reviewer reported none within the stated coverage.
- Report what changed, what was verified, and any remaining limitation. An attempted command or passing syntax check is not proof of the intended effect.
